The idea

Two-date differencing asks "did this pixel change between date A and date B?" — and fires on seasonality, water-level swings and single cloudy scenes. This system instead learns what each pixel normally does across the year, then asks "has this pixel departed from its own normal behaviour, and has that departure persisted?"

1 · Normal-behaviour models (run in parallel and compared)

  • Seasonal harmonic model: NDVI ≈ pixel level + a₁sin + a₂cos + a₃sin2 + a₄cos2 over day-of-year. Each pixel keeps its own level; the seasonal shape is pooled across all pixels (tens of thousands of training observations), which stays stable when a normal period has few scenes — a purely per-pixel harmonic overfits there, and we show that in the methods note.
  • MLP regressor: scikit-learn MLPRegressor (64–32 ReLU, Adam, squared error, early stopping). Inputs: seasonal harmonics + the pixel's normal-period median NDVI, NDWI and MNDWI. Output: expected NDVI.

2 · Persistence engine

Residual = observed − expected NDVI. In the monitoring period a pixel is anomalous when |residual| ≥ threshold. It becomes a persistent change only after k consecutive anomalous clear observations. Threshold and k are tuned on tuning blocks only. We record onset date, longest run, anomaly count, mean magnitude and a 0–1 confidence score.

3 · Change typing & mapping

Monitoring-vs-normal shifts in NDVI, MNDWI, NDMI and BSI classify each pixel (vegetation gain/loss, water gain/loss, vegetation & water loss, built-up/bare encroachment, mixed). Connected pixels (≥9 px ≈ 0.09 ha) become polygons with hectares, onset, and confidence.

4 · Baseline

The conventional comparator: |NDVI difference| between the clearest normal-period and clearest monitoring-period scenes, threshold tuned the same way. It has no seasonal model and no persistence requirement.
